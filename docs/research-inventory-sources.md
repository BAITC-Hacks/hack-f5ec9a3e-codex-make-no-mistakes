## Recommendation

Keep the selected capped-EWMA forecast as the control. The highest-value next experiment is an end-to-end replenishment comparison at the actual lead-time-plus-review horizon, with dated arrivals and explicit inventory assumptions. ## Minimal candidate policy and uncertainty experiment

This is a proposed experiment, not measured superiority.

1. Pin L, R, day convention, stock scope, arrival completeness, reservation semantics and lost-sales versus backorder treatment. Keep these identical across forecast candidates. Under the current proposed backorder convention, the aggregate raw need is max(0, forecast over L+R + buffer - free stock - eligible inbound). Evaluate the daily trajectory too: a receipt near the end of the horizon cannot serve an earlier shortage. A standard inventory-position identity is not permission to count undated or late inbound as immediately available.
2. Compare the existing explicit buffer against a calibrated protection-period residual buffer. For each past origin, calculate actual cumulative sales minus the cumulative forecast available at that origin over the same horizon. Estimate the selected upper residual quantile using only earlier residuals. Treat this initially as sales-based uncertainty, not unconstrained-demand uncertainty. Pool sparse SKUs only by a predeclared, documented rule; report performance for sparse cohorts rather than imply reliable per-SKU tail estimation.
3. Use direct cumulative-horizon quantiles as a later challenger if calibration and inventory performance justify them. Do not add an independently calculated safety stock on top of an already service-targeted demand quantile: that would generally count uncertainty twice. Do not sum daily marginal quantiles and describe the sum as a calibrated horizon quantile.
4. Vary lead-time assumptions separately from demand uncertainty. With no empirical lead-time history, show explicit on-time/delayed receipt scenarios and order-date sensitivity; do not assign a learned delay distribution. Once order/receipt history exists, evaluate joint demand/arrival uncertainty and preserve any relevant dependence.
5. Apply confirmed unit conversion, minimum and multiple rules to every candidate before comparing final recommendations. Evaluate post-rounding excess and order volatility: MOQ can dominate a small improvement in statistical forecast error.

## Evaluation and promotion gates

All numerical thresholds below must be agreed before looking at results; none is an established business target.

| Gate | Required evidence | What passing permits |
|---|---|---|
| Input validity | Dated authoritative stock, receipt status/ETA/scope, unit conversions, quantity-rule meaning, explicit completeness and reservation semantics. Unknowns retain their status. | Actionable rows can be reviewed. Missing inputs still permit labelled scenarios, not fabricated ready quantities. |
| Policy correctness | Existing Q1–Q11 contract cases plus distinct lost-sales/backorder scenarios, same-day arrivals, partial receipts, late receipts, and a no-demand case. Conservation and rounding identities hold. | Claim deterministic arithmetic works under stated conventions. |
| Forecast and uncertainty validity | Frozen cumulative-horizon forecasts and past-only residual calibration; bias/WAPE, pinball loss and empirical quantile coverage by supplier/activity cohort. Handle all-zero actuals explicitly. | Claim improvement on observed sales/uncertainty; no automatic inventory claim. |
| Simulation robustness | Identical starting state, demand path and arrival assumptions across policies; report filled units, unmet units, average stock, stockout days, terminal inventory/backlog, order frequency and rounding excess. Distinguish lost units from backorder unit-days. Include delayed arrival and missing-availability sensitivity. | Claim relative scenario performance. Historical observed sales replay must be labelled a constrained-demand proxy. |
| Business promotion | At agreed service targets compare inventory/service frontiers; or, if real costs are available, predeclare cost objective and service guardrails. Paired uncertainty intervals must preserve time dependence/overlapping horizon effects. Require gains across important supplier/activity cohorts and a fresh forward/shadow period. | Candidate can become recommended policy after human review; monetary or realized-service claims require observed business outcomes. |

Do not sum mixed stock units across items as though meters and pieces were interchangeable. With unconfirmed cost meanings, use item-level stock/service measures and transparently normalized aggregates or cost-ratio sensitivity, not currency savings. Compute unit fill rate as units immediately served divided by demand units; cycle service as cycles with no shortage divided by evaluated cycles. Label the latter separately from fill rate. At the terminal horizon, include remaining stock and outstanding backlog so a policy cannot appear better by deferring costs past the evaluation boundary.

Since the 2026 retrospective forecast results are already known, fresh tuning on them must be called retrospective development. A future untouched period or predeclared shadow evaluation is needed for a clean promotion check. Existing 7/28-day sales archives can help with controls where horizons match; they do not by themselves supply arbitrary L+R forecasts, historical stock, complete receipts or latent demand.

## Censoring: what to do now and what to collect

Now: expose an availability-data status. Keep observed gross-positive-sales evaluation reproducible; run labelled synthetic known-demand/known-stockout acceptance cases and sensitivity scenarios separately. Do not replace monthly blanks with zero stock, inflate every zero-sales day, or call reconstructed demand observed truth. Returns and adjustments require separate inventory-flow semantics even when the forecast target remains gross positive sales.

Collect first: SKU/warehouse/unit, timestamped usable on-hand or availability intervals, receipts and adjustments, reservation creation/release/fulfillment, customer order requests and cancellations/backorders where available, and supplier order/confirmed/actual receipt dates including partial receipts. Explicitly distinguish unavailable data from an observed zero event count.

Then: compare a simple availability-aware baseline with any censored-demand estimator. Mask known demand under controlled synthetic stockouts to check recoverability; on real data use genuinely observed availability and forward outcomes. Evaluating an imputer against its own imputed target is circular. Excluding unavailable days can help a baseline but does not guarantee unbiased demand: demand itself can cause stockouts. Any correction must disclose assumptions about substitution, backorders, ordering behavior and unobserved availability.
