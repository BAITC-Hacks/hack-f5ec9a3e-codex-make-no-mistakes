# **Case. Automating Supplier Order Generation**

| 1\. Name | Automatic calculation of supplier orders for warehouse replenishment |
| :---- | :---- |
| **2\. Problem and Value** | Currently, a purchasing department manager calculates warehouse replenishment needs manually, consolidating data in Excel. Because such detailed analysis is labor-intensive, the calculation is done infrequently and not in real time. This leads to excess stock for some items and shortages for others, while one-off large orders (including high-volume sales to a single customer) distort the calculation of regular demand, resulting in unnecessary storage costs and lost sales. **Value of the solution:** lower excess storage costs, fewer shortages and lost sales, and more accurate regular purchasing decisions. |
| **3\. User** | **Primary user:** purchasing department manager. **Key scenario:** runs a calculation for a warehouse or category → receives a list of recommended supplier orders with a rationale for each item → reviews and adjusts if necessary → approves the order. |
| **4\. Task** | Develop a service that generates recommended supplier orders based on sales history, seasonality, sustained demand growth, current stock levels, goods in transit, product categories, a growth forecast, and an estimate of lost demand during out-of-stock periods. The service must detect one-off large orders (including large sales to a single customer) and exclude them from the calculation of regular stock requirements. |
| **5\. Input → Output** | **Input:** sales history for the period (date, SKU, quantity, anonymized customer, price), current stock levels by warehouse, out-of-stock periods (stockout) by SKU, a directory of suppliers and lead times, and a bill of materials from 1C. **Output:** a list of recommended orders (SKU, supplier, recommended quantity, rationale, urgency) as a table/dashboard with export capability. |
| **6\. Data (if the task is data-based)** | **Link/access method:** an export of sales history and stock levels; the partner will provide the V2 report and sales dynamics data (a sample is provided by the partner; the full dataset and exact release date are subject to agreement). **Fields and volume:** sale date, SKU, product name, quantity, price, anonymized customer ID, warehouse, stock level as of date; sales history over an extended period (the order of magnitude will be confirmed by the partner). **Constraints:** customer data must be anonymized; synthetic data with a realistic distribution may be used for development and testing. |
| **7\. Must have** | 1\) Calculation of baseline replenishment needs for each SKU, taking into account all provided data (sales history, current stock, goods in transit, product categories, growth forecast) — verification: on the test dataset, a correct list of items with recommended quantities is generated, and the calculation does not ignore any of the supplied data sources — a change in any of them (for example, the volume of goods in transit) is reflected in the final result. 2\) Accounting for seasonality and sustained demand growth in forecasting — verification: for a product with pronounced seasonality, the forecast reflects the seasonal pattern rather than simply the average over the entire history. 3\) Estimation and compensation of lost demand during out-of-stock periods — verification: for an SKU with a recorded stockout, the calculated requirement is adjusted upward compared to a calculation based on "raw" actual sales. 4\) Detection and exclusion of one-off large orders, including large sales to a single customer — verification: a one-off large order artificially added to the test data does not cause a significant increase in the recommended regular quantity. 5\) Generation of a final order list grouped by supplier, with a brief rationale for each item — verification: every row of the output list comes with an explanation of why exactly that quantity is proposed, and the list can be viewed broken down by supplier. |
| **8\. Optional** | Prioritizing items by shortage risk; accounting for minimum order quantities and specific supplier terms; visualizing demand trends by category; automatic export/sending of the generated order to the supplier. |
| **9\. Constraints** | **Not allowed:** automatically sending an order to a supplier without confirmation from the responsible employee; using non-anonymized customer data in calculations. **Must take into account:** privacy of sales and customer data, explainability of the calculation, robustness of the algorithm to anomalies and outliers in the input data, compatibility of the output format with the company's current accounting system. |
| **10\. Deliverables** | Repository: yes. README: yes (calculation methodology, description of the outlier exclusion algorithm, launch instructions). |
